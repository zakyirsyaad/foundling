# Foundling threat model

This document is the English threat model for Foundling, a USDC vault on NEAR whose trading strategy runs sealed inside an OutLayer TEE while the vault contract enforces caps, exit and roles. It is a translation and condensation of four sections of `docs/SPEC.md`, which is written in Indonesian and remains the source. It lists who holds which role, the invariants the contract enforces, the parties that still have to be trusted, and each known threat with its mitigation and stated residual risk. Where the SPEC text is older than the deployed vault, the account and key facts below are the ones read on chain on 2026-11-13, and each such place is marked "current state".

Terms used below:

- **Lead** and **partner**: the two members of the team. **FCK**: a NEAR function-call access key.
- **Agent**: the strategy WASM running on OutLayer. **Keeper**: the account that triggers cycles.
- **Anchor**: the price read from `aggregate-oracle.near`. **Rhea**: the swap venue (`v2.ref-finance.near`). **Window**: the rolling `epoch_len` window (6 hours in the SPEC's numbers).
- **Signing room**: in the SPEC, a second macOS user account that holds the parent, governor and depositor keys and has no toolchain, repo, bot or agent. **Workspace**: the lead's development environment.

## Current state on 2026-11-13

The SPEC describes the intended setup. Three points differ on the deployed vault today, and the tables below should be read with them in mind:

- `open_deposits` is true: any account registered through `storage_deposit` can deposit, within `tvl_cap` (100 USDC) and `per_user_cap` (50 USDC).
- The secrets-owner account `sec.foundling.near` still has its full-access key. Until that key is deleted, threat 9 applies in full: the lead can delete the secrets row or change its access conditions.
- The signing room is not in use for this deployment. The parent, governor, secrets-owner and depositor keys sit in the lead's main user account, on the same machine as the workspace. Worst-case row 10 and threat 26 assume the separation; without it, a compromise of that machine is worst-case row 11.

## Roles

Current state: the final vault is `vault.foundling.near` on NEAR mainnet and has no access keys. Governor is `gov.foundling.near`, keeper is `keeper.foundling.near`, guardian is `guard.foundling.near`. The vault was created as a sub-account in one transaction signed by the parent account (CreateAccount, Transfer, DeployContract, FunctionCall `init`, no AddKey).

| Role | Who holds it | What it can do | What it cannot do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strategy Creator | Holds the parameters and salt (OutLayer secrets), the lead's parent-account key, and the secrets-owner account key (deleted after a successful post-seal cycle) | Writes the strategy WASM (built in amd64 CI), fills in the secret parameters, publishes the commitment, and runs the one transaction that creates the sealed vault. `init` runs once, inside that transaction, and is rejected unless the predecessor is the parent of the vault account name | Has no power over the vault contract after it is created. Until the secrets-owner key is deleted, it can still delete the secrets (the agent stops permanently) or change the secrets ACL |
| Depositor | Holds shares | Deposits USDC (through `ft_on_transfer`, only from the USDC contract; must be registered with `storage_deposit` and on the allowlist, or anyone when `open_deposits`; not while frozen or halted; subject to `tvl_cap`, `per_user_cap`, `min_deposit`; the deposit enters a queue). `cancel_deposit` at any time. `redeem(shares)`, `withdraw_claimable`, `claim_bucket` at every stage (I4). A `redeem` whose payouts are all 0 reverts | - |
| Guardian | `guard.foundling.near`, held by the partner on the partner's own device. Current state: the account has exactly one key, an FCK limited to `freeze` and `cancel_keeper_change` on the vault; its full-access key was deleted | `freeze` and `cancel_keeper_change`, at any time | Unfreeze, change policy, move funds. It is replaced through `schedule_guardian_change` (governor, timelock) |
| Governor | `gov.foundling.near`, an ordinary account owned by the lead (lead decision 2026-11-03; no SputnikDAO). It is not the parent account | `unfreeze`, `clear_halt`, `tighten_policy`, `add_depositor`, `schedule_keeper_change`, `schedule_guardian_change`. A keeper, signer or guardian rotation takes effect only after `timelock`; a guardian rotation cannot be cancelled by the old guardian; a new schedule replaces the old one and emits an event. `tighten_policy` moves each parameter only in its tightening direction and not past its floor or ceiling (caps not below 10% of their initial value). `open_deposits` can only be closed | Loosen caps, add assets or routes, replace the strategy, upgrade, move funds, reveal the secrets |
| Keeper | `keeper.foundling.near`, held by the lead. Current state: an FCK on the keeper server limited to `trigger_cycle`, `execute_decision`, `advance`, `abort_stale_cycle`, `expire_decision`, plus a full-access key held by the lead | `trigger_cycle` (stage Idle, cooldown, `ops_budget`, not frozen, minimum prepaid gas) and `execute_decision` (stage DecisionReady, not frozen, minimum prepaid gas). The SPEC also gives the keeper `submit_decision` in its fallback mode A only (stage `AwaitingDecision`, matching nonce, valid ed25519 signature over the decision digest against `signer_pubkey`); the current keeper FCK does not include that method | Make decisions; move funds |
| Agent | The OutLayer WASM with the installed hash. Holds secrets bound to that hash, read inside the enclave | Returns a decision to the vault | Touch vault funds |
| Verifier | Anyone, through the CLI, `verify.py`, or the static proof page | - | - |
| Anyone | - | `setup_storage` (once per target: USDC, wNEAR, Rhea; uses the vault's own NEAR), `selftest` (final once passed), the NEP-145 storage methods (fixed registration fee of 0.015 NEAR, registration is permanent), `advance`, `abort_stale_cycle`, `expire_decision`, `apply_keeper_change`, `apply_guardian_change`, `reveal`, each under stage and time conditions. Deposits are rejected until `setup_storage` and `selftest` have completed | - |

No public method exists in the contract outside the ones named above. Private callbacks (`#[private]`, only from the vault's own promises) are `on_decision`, `on_price`, `on_deposited`, `on_swapped`, `on_withdrawn`, `on_probe`, `on_xfer`, `on_setup`, `on_selftest`. The only foreign entry point is `ft_on_transfer`.

The team is two people. The lead holds governor and keeper; the partner holds guardian. The partner can stop cycles and delay a keeper rotation; the lead can unfreeze immediately and replace the guardian after `timelock`. This is a separation of duties inside one team. It limits what one leaked key can do; it does not limit the lead's device or intent. The partner does not hold a keeper key on purpose: if guardian and keeper were one person, one compromised device could both trigger cycles and cancel the rotation to a replacement keeper. The keeper must be an account separate from the vault. Roles are bound to account ids, not to keys.

## Invariants

Each invariant has a named test in CI (near-workspaces, `testing_env!` with proptest on the accounting core, a mock venue built on NEAR's promise suspend/resume mechanism, and sandbox tests). Random interleaving between hops is tested at unit level and in the mock venue, not against the real Rhea. `#[private]` is only active at the WASM entry points, so `attack forged-caller` is tested in the sandbox, not in unit tests.

| # | Invariant | Tested through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I1 | The share price does not fall because of rounding; `redeem(deposit(x)) ≤ x`; a virtual offset prevents the inflation attack (the warning in the NEP-621 document); donations do not change the share price, except output dust on a swap whose response was lost (step S of `advance`), which can only raise it | proptest; `attack inflation`, `donate-dust` |
| I2 | Buy: `amount_in` ≤ `cap`, cumulative within the rolling `epoch_len` window ≤ `epoch_cap`. Sell: `amount_in` ≤ `cap_wnear`, ≤ `cap_sell_bps` of the wNEAR book (except a 100% sweep when `wnear_book` ≤ `cap_wnear`), cumulative ≤ `epoch_cap_wnear`. Gap between trades ≥ `cooldown`. wNEAR exposure after a buy ≤ `max_risk_bps`. A sell that lowers exposure is never blocked by the exposure limit | `attack over-cap`, `drip`, `never-flat` |
| I3 | Assets ∈ {USDC, wNEAR}; routes ∈ registered pools; NAV, caps and `min_amount_out` use the anchor price read with `age` = `price_max_age`, non-null, `publish_time` ≤ now + 5 seconds, the same `expo` on both feeds, and `conf/price` ≤ `price_max_conf_bps` | `attack bad-route`, `stale-price` |
| I4 | Pro-rata in-kind `redeem` is available at every cycle stage, while frozen, while halted, and without the keeper or OutLayer; qualifications per asset class are in the table below | sandbox "redeem at every stage"; `attack kill-agent` |
| I5 | Immutable core: no upgrade method and no access key; policy can only be tightened (with a floor or ceiling for every parameter), and `open_deposits` only changes from open to closed; keeper, signer and guardian rotations are timelocked; a keeper rotation can be cancelled by the guardian | `verify keys`; `attack governor-loosen` |
| I6 | Gross operational spending (the amount attached to OutLayer calls) ≤ `ops_budget` per window | sandbox |
| I7 | One nonce, one execution | `attack replay` |
| I8 | Async accounting and liveness: the books change only from returned values; books and shares are debited before the promise leaves; `rhea_bal` is tracked and `Idle` means `rhea_bal` = 0; a failed transfer credits `claimable` exactly; at most one swap in flight; Σ bucket claims ≤ proceeds; shares are minted only when the vault is flat (`wnear_book` ≤ `dust_wnear`); no callback panics and each carries (nonce, stage, attempt); every path returns to Idle or to a `Stuck` state that can be retried | proptest; a mock OutLayer built on the real suspend/resume mechanism; `attack double-redeem`, `donate-dust`, `queue-stuff`, `advance-vs-redeem`, `advance-race`, `never-flat` |
| I9 | Outgoing NEP-141 transfers go only to a share owner who redeems, a claim owner, or `v2.ref-finance.near` (the deposit step); calls to Rhea are only `swap`, `withdraw`, `storage_deposit` (and the view `get_deposit`); NEAR leaves only to `outlayer.near`, and to the token contracts and Rhea for 1 yoctoNEAR and `storage_deposit` | sandbox |
| I10 | `decision.commitment == active_commitment`, recomputed by the WASM from its secrets; the commitment and the decision digest are bound to the vault, the chain and `wasm_hash`. This is a consistency check, not a security boundary | sandbox |
| I11 | The vault's NEAR balance minus operational spending ≥ storage stake + `near_reserve`, so that state writes (including `redeem`) do not fail | sandbox at the floor boundary |
| I12 | While frozen or halted: `trigger_cycle`, `execute_decision`, new swaps and new deposits are rejected; a swap that has not started is unwound (`Depositing` + frozen → `Withdrawing`, unwind) and a swap already in flight is completed without a new trade; all other methods keep working, including redeem, claims, `advance`, `abort_stale_cycle`, `expire_decision`, `cancel_deposit`, `reveal` and `apply_*`; only the governor unfreezes | `attack freeze-race`, `freeze-reveal`, `guardian-self-protect` |
| I13 | Absolute caps per incoming token (`cap_usdc` for buys, `cap_wnear` for sells) apply regardless of price; the smaller of the absolute cap and the bps cap wins | `attack inflated-nav`, `deflated-sell` |

Exit path per position of funds:

| Where the funds are | Exit path without the agent | Depends on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| USDC on the books | `redeem` directly, in kind | The USDC contract (the issuer can block or pause); the redeemer is registered on the token, or the balance becomes `claimable` |
| wNEAR on the books | Paid in kind, pro rata | The wNEAR contract (no keys and no upgrade); the redeemer is registered, or `claimable` |
| A swap in flight (at most one) | `claim_bucket(nonce)` after the books are credited; if `Stuck`, `advance`; if Rhea is paused, the funds wait in Rhea's internal account until the Rhea owner unpauses | Rhea (its guardian can pause and only its owner unpauses); the value is ≤ one cap |
| A queued deposit | `cancel_deposit` | - |
| Assets on another chain | Outside the vault: the user swaps on their own through 1Click from their own account | 1Click and its terms of service |

## Who is still trusted

The SPEC names four properties, G1 to G4. For each, these parties must still be trusted:

| Property | Parties that must still be trusted |
| --- | --- |
| G1 confidentiality | Intel TDX and NVIDIA; the Phala host; the OutLayer operator (`worker.outlayer.near` holds 3 full-access keys; the list of 66 measurements does not bind the holders of those keys); the keystore's access-condition logic and MPC-CKD; NEAR AI Cloud (the prompt is visible inside its TEE); the creator (the owner of the OutLayer secrets row can call `update_access` and `delete_secrets` until its key is deleted). Physical attacks on the TDX host (TEE.fail, 2025; a DDR5 interposer under US$1,000) are outside Intel's threat model. Execution inputs (`input_data`, `secrets_ref`, signer_id, predecessor) and execution events are public on chain, the secrets ciphertext stays on chain forever, and a prompt compiled into the public WASM is not secret. The leak per decision shrinks ≈2/(N+1) for passive inputs, up to 2^-N if an attacker shifts the inputs (for example the pool price) |
| G2 bounded loss | The vault code (tested by the team, no third-party audit). The aggregate oracle `aggregate-oracle.near` v0.4.1: a Rhea contract with private code that combines sources per token (nine global sources; for wNEAR: Pyth Pro mandatory, plus Binance, CoinGecko, Atlas and OutLayer, with a valid price range of US$0.30–10.00 and a maximum deviation of 100 bps). It has no access keys but can be upgraded and paused instantly by two SputnikDAOs with the same council (`bim.near`, `brhost.near`, `nicktrust.near`, threshold 2 of 3). Two of those council members also have roles at Rhea (`nicktrust.near` on the five-member council of the owner DAO with a 3-vote threshold, `bim.near` as guardian), so the anchor and the swap venue are one trust domain. The influence of the `outlayer` source on the aggregate price is not yet known. The Rhea contract with its owner and guardian (who can pause). The guardian key (partner) and the keeper key (lead) for the speed of a freeze. The governor (lead) only for unfreeze, keeper or guardian rotation, and tightening. Lead and partner are one team, so this is a separation of duties inside the team, not between separate parties. There is no other price fallback: without a valid anchor there is no trade |
| G3 exit path | The vault code; the USDC contract (issuer: blocklister, pausers, `upgrade`, 1 full-access key) and the wNEAR contract (no keys and no upgrade); NEAR itself; Rhea only for the portion that is in a swap. It does not depend on the agent, the keeper, OutLayer or the governor |
| G4 traceability | The verifier (open source); OutLayer's result for the installed hash (the OutLayer operator holds the keys); NEAR AI for the inference signature (it shows that one of the model instances sharing the signing key served that request). Attestation proves the Intel TDX hardware and NVIDIA GPU (DCAP and NRAS), the dstack OS, and the compose at boot (RTMR0–3 from event-log replay, `compose_hash`), but the model workload is installed at runtime by NEAR AI's compose-manager and is recorded only as file hashes and commits in the attested action log, so the inference image and the model weights remain trusted to NEAR AI (tested 2026-11-07, GLM and Qwen). The reproducible build. Decision inputs are recorded in `obs`, so a replay checks the rules, not the execution |

**What sealing ensures and what it does not.** Being created sealed ensures there is no admin key and no upgrade on the vault (after an allowlist-based audit of imports); tighten-only ensures there is no loosening. Neither ensures that no third party can direct funds: authority moves to the dependencies that are not sealed, namely the oracle DAO with instant upgrade, the Rhea owner and guardian, the OutLayer owner and operator, the creator's secrets row, and the USDC issuer. No party controlled by the vault can move funds to a destination other than share owners and the registered token contracts or Rhea, or beyond the caps; outside parties (the USDC issuer, Rhea, the oracle DAO) can hold funds that are in transit or influence the price that is used.

**Worst case in numbers.** These are the SPEC's demo defaults: TVL 100 USDC, NAV 100, so for buys `cap` = min(10% × 100, 15) = 10 USDC and `epoch_cap` = min(20% × 100, 30) = 20 USDC; sells are limited by `cap_wnear` 4 and `epoch_cap_wnear` 8 (≈US$18 and ≈US$36 at US$4.5); `max_risk_bps` 50%; `slippage_bps` 150 already includes the 30 bps pool fee; anchor error e ≈ 125 bps (the `price_max_conf_bps` limit of 50 plus a p99 drift of 65 bps in 2 minutes; a 24-hour measurement found NEAR `conf` at p95 17.6 bps and maximum 61.8 bps, and the vault rejects samples above the limit). Bound per window = (`epoch_cap` + value of `epoch_cap_wnear`) × (`slippage_bps` + e). The lean budget puts only 10–20 USDC of demo capital in: the bps caps bind, rows 1 and 2 shrink in proportion to NAV, while in row 3 the whole 10–20 USDC can be lost in one window. The table uses TVL 100 as the parameter ceiling. The SPEC states that the README carries this table with numbers from the deployed parameters, computed by `verify`.

| # | Scenario | Bound | Formula or note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Threats and mitigations

| # | Threat | Mitigation and residual risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A function-call key is assumed to limit amounts | Amount limits are in the contract (I2, I13); the FCKs only limit which methods the keeper and guardian can call |
| 2 | The agent supplies a destination address (for example a `depositAddress` from a quote) | The vault only sends to registered destinations; the route and `min_amount_out` are computed by the contract (I3, I9); `attack bad-route` |
| 3 | Swap accounting from balance differences, donations, or transfers that cross each other | The books change only from returned values; the `rhea_bal` ledger; donations do not enter the books (I8), including donations to the vault's internal account at Rhea through `mft_transfer`, which are limited at the probe step. One exception: output dust on a swap whose response was lost cannot be told apart from proceeds, so it enters the books as a gift to share holders and the donor loses it; `attack donate-dust` |
| 4 | A Rhea `withdraw` looks successful although the transfer to the vault failed (the callback re-credits and returns `"0"`) | `on_withdrawn` credits the books only from the amount delivered; 0 or partial → `Stuck(W)`; never send an amount of 0 (I8) |
| 5 | Storage spam on an open vault: new accounts deposit and then `cancel_deposit` repeatedly until the storage stake breaks through `near_reserve`, blocking deposits and `trigger_cycle` | A fixed-fee NEP-145 registration that covers every entry a depositor can hold (unit test `the_fee_covers_every_entry_a_depositor_can_hold`, sandbox `deposit_then_cancel_costs_the_vault_no_free_near`). Registration is permanent, so the fee cannot be withdrawn and reused. Bucket claims are limited to one per account: an old claim is merged into `claimable` when the account calls `redeem` during the next swap, and a bucket is deleted when its claims are used up |
| 6 | The OutLayer worker is compromised or returns a fake result | Only the operator can resume the suspended promise; caps, routes and the anchor price limit the loss (G2); the verifier checks attestation off chain; trust in the operator key is stated (see "Who is still trusted"); the checks on commitment, `ref_price` and the `wasm_hash` echo are consistency checks, not a security boundary |
| 7 | The operator or runner reads the strategy | Parameters exist only inside the enclave; only the creator sees them. Trade patterns and public inputs leak the rules statistically, up to 2^-N if an attacker shifts the inputs (SPEC §2.1); the real parameters are not used in the public soak; no secret threshold depends on the pool spot price |
| 8 | Threshold extraction with synthetic inputs (another caller, a relay, the keeper, or HTTPS mode) | ACL `And[Predecessor{Whitelist vault}, WasmHash]` (without `Whitelist[keeper]`, so a keeper rotation does not touch the ACL); the `callers` manifest inside the WASM rejects the `direct` and `https` doors before decryption; `input_data` is serialized with serde and contains only vault state; the strategy is not exposed over HTTPS; the leak per public decision is to be recomputed in SPEC milestone M2 |
| 9 | The creator changes or deletes the secrets row after sealing (`update_access`, `delete_secrets`) | The secrets-owner account is a sub-account whose key is deleted after a successful post-seal cycle; `verify keys` covers it; before that the creator can delete the row (permanent denial of service for the agent) or loosen the ACL, and the README states this |
| 10 | Replay of an old decision or result, including across vaults or networks | Monotonic nonce, expiry enforced by the contract, decision digest and commitment bound to the vault, the chain and `wasm_hash` (I7, I10) |
| 11 | `verify replay` is circular because its inputs are supplied by the creator | `obs` (prices from two sources, timestamps, and the AI Risk Manager output, which the SPEC calls the tripwire output) is in the on-chain Decision and `inputs_digest` binds it; `tools/recheck_obs.py` re-fetches `pa` from pool 4512 at the block in second `ta` through an archival RPC and `pb` from the 20 Kraken trades ending at second `tb`, without keys; a source that cannot be read becomes `SKIP` |
| 12 | Prompt injection or poisoning from a feed | The LLM (the AI Risk Manager) can only lower exposure: shrink or hold back a buy, or trigger a protective sell to USDC on severe risk; it cannot start or enlarge a buy. This is carried out by the installed WASM; the contract cannot check it. Two price sources must match; the decision schema is enforced by the contract with rejection, not clamping |
| 13 | The fail-safe is used as a lever (making the two sources disagree to trigger a sell) | A mismatch produces `NoDecision`, not `RiskOff`; every trade counts against the window caps |
| 14 | A `null`, `Failed` or timed-out callback leaves the vault stuck | Callbacks do not panic and read results with `promise_result_checked`; `abort_stale_cycle`; `advance` with the `Probing` stage; `redeem` does not depend on cycle status (I4, I8) |
| 15 | Stale, fake or controlled price | The `aggregate-oracle.near` anchor is read with `age` = `price_max_age` through the vault's own `.then`; a stale feed returns `null` → `PRICE_UNAVAILABLE`; price > 0, same `expo`, `publish_time` ≤ now + 5 seconds, bounded `conf`; absolute caps (I13). Residual: the anchor is controlled by a DAO that can upgrade it instantly and is not separate from Rhea; there is no price fallback |
| 16 | Drip-drain and bursts at an epoch boundary | Half-open rolling window (`t > now − epoch_len`) and cooldown (I2) |
| 17 | NAV dilution or sandwich at mint; the vault is never flat | Shares are minted only when the vault is flat (`wnear_book` ≤ `dust_wnear`), at book NAV with no price involved; a 100% sell is allowed when `wnear_book` ≤ `cap_wnear` (I8); `attack inflation`, `never-flat` |
| 18 | NEP-141 storage registration is missing; output gets stuck in Rhea's lostfound; `setup_storage` is spammed | `setup_storage` is single-use per target with a flag and a fixed amount, and both tokens are registered on the internal Rhea account; `claimable` (I8, I9); `attack setup-spam` |
| 19 | Operational spending drains the vault's NEAR until state writes fail | Gross `ops_budget` per window, `near_reserve` floor, `trigger_cycle` only by the keeper (I6, I11) |
| 20 | The governor loosens policy, or a typo shuts trading down | There is no loosening method; `tighten_policy` has a floor or ceiling for every parameter (not only the caps); a keeper rotation is timelocked and can be cancelled by the guardian (I5) |
| 21 | An upgrade or migration locks the vault | No upgrade and no access keys; a bug is handled with a new vault, and users exit through `redeem` |
| 22 | The window before sealing is used to seize `init`; `init` is called with valid but wrong arguments | The vault account does not exist before the creating transaction and all its actions are atomic; `init` rejects any predecessor other than the parent account and cannot be called again; the unsealed twin only holds team funds within budget; `Initialized{args_hash}`, `selftest`, and a per-field `verify config` against the twin before deposits open (must match: roles, policy, assets, `price_ids`, pool, WASM hash and URL, timelock; must differ: vault name, commitment, ACL; the arguments are decoded from the transaction bytes) |
| 23 | Hidden keys (new key types such as ML-DSA) or dangerous host functions | `verify keys` fails on unknown key types and on WASM imports outside the allowlist (nearcore has 16 account-changing imports; a four-name denylist is not enough), and the WASM exports are matched exactly against the SPEC §3.1 method matrix plus callbacks and views |
| 24 | The guardian is misused, or its key leaks | It can only `freeze` and cancel a keeper rotation; it cannot unfreeze; `redeem` keeps working; `schedule_guardian_change` (timelock, cannot be cancelled by the old guardian); the FCK has no allowance and `verify` prints the balance against the gas-purchase requirement. Current state: the SPEC says the guardian's full-access key is kept offline; that key has since been deleted and the account holds only the FCK |

**Key-leak runbook.** Roles are accounts, so a key can be revoked without touching the vault.

| Event | First response | If that fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keeper FCK leaked | The lead uses `DeleteKey` on the FCK and installs a new one; the partner freezes if the lead is not available | `schedule_keeper_change` |
| Keeper full-access key leaked | The partner freezes; the lead calls `schedule_keeper_change` | Rotation after `timelock` |
| Guardian FCK leaked | SPEC: the partner uses `DeleteKey` on the FCK from the offline key; the lead unfreezes. Current state: the offline full-access key no longer exists, so this first step does not apply | `schedule_guardian_change` |
| Guardian full-access key leaked | SPEC: the lead calls `schedule_guardian_change` and unfreezes once the guardian is replaced. Current state: this key was deleted | `redeem` keeps working |
| Partner cannot be reached | The lead stops the bot, uses `DeleteKey` on the keeper FCK, and calls `tighten_policy` | Trading stops |
| Governor key leaked | The partner freezes; the lead adds a new key on the governor account and deletes the old one | A race for the keys; a new vault |
